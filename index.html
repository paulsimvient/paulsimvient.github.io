System architecture

Six layers. One evidence chain.

The system keeps world state, competing explanations, intervention logic, validation, retained knowledge, and human review separate enough to inspect and test.

01 / DOMAIN

Represent the world

Models, scenarios, observations, actions, outcomes, constraints, and true state.

WorldState → ObservableState(agent, condition)
02 / STRATEGY

Carry alternatives

Maintain competing predictive models instead of collapsing early to one explanation.

{H1,H2,H3} → predictions(e)
03 / EXPERIMENT

Intervene deliberately

Pair control and treatment with matched seeds and explicit intervention manifests.

run(S,HOLD) ↔ run(S,E1)
04 / VALIDATION

Freeze and test

Separate discovery from confirmation. Hold out conditions. Quantify uncertainty.

discovery ≠ confirmation
05 / KNOWLEDGE

Retain limits

Store claims with provenance, contradictions, uncertainty, and validity envelope intact.

claim + evidence + boundary
06 / WORKBENCH

Keep the human in control

Expose proposals, evidence, unresolved differences, and experiment history for review.

propose → inspect → approve/reject

Thinking Together

Why build it this way?

Language models can give unfinished reasoning the surface form of finished reasoning. A qualified result can become an unqualified sentence. Dependent reports can become “multiple sources.” A simulation can become “what will happen.” A hypothesis can become “the cause.”

The book develops the human side of the same problem: how people and machines can investigate together without losing provenance, alternatives, uncertainty, or responsibility.
