Using markets as a laboratory

The question is not whether an AI agent can place a bid. The question is what controlled experiments reveal about how its behavior changes when information, incentives, market history, and peer activity change.

The problem

Black-box agents can produce the same action for very different reasons. One model may react to recent price movement. Another may respond to news. A third may follow peers. In one market state, those explanations can fit the same bid.

That makes the problem experimental. A useful benchmark has to create conditions in which plausible explanations make different predictions, then observe what the agent actually does.

A treatment effect tells us that behavior changed. It does not, by itself, tell us which explanation is correct.

A practical experimental sequence

The market is useful because the underlying state, information shown to each agent, and resulting actions can be separated and controlled.

01 · QUALIFY

Prove the market first

Verify auction mechanics, accounting, payouts, gains from trade, and allocative efficiency with programmed agents whose rules are known in advance.

02 · CONTROL

Separate true state from observed state

Keep private value, cost, cash, and inventory distinct from the news, price history, and peer behavior an agent is allowed to see.

03 · COMPETE

Write down rival explanations

Represent candidate behavioral models as observable predictions. Do not infer hidden beliefs or psychology from a convincing pattern.

04 · INTERVENE

Change one source of information

Vary news reliability, timing, market history, or peer visibility while holding the underlying economic fundamentals fixed where the design allows.

05 · PAIR

Use matched runs

When treatment and control can share exogenous randomness, use the same seed so the comparison isolates the intervention from avoidable run-to-run variation.

06 · HOLD OUT

Confirm on unseen conditions

Freeze the model, outcomes, thresholds, exclusions, and stopping rules before confirmation. Test on reserved valuations, histories, mechanisms, and multi-agent conditions.

Example experiment

News × price history

Suppose private value, cash, quantity constraints, and the market fundamental stay fixed. The experiment changes only two things: whether the agent sees favorable news and whether recent prices are stable or rising.

That creates four cells. If the news effect is much larger after a rising price history than after a stable history, the interaction is evidence that the response to news depends on recent market context.

The result still has limits. It does not establish why the model behaved that way. It establishes a reproducible, observable dependency that can be tested again.

Measure behavior without inventing motives

The benchmark stays close to what can actually be observed and reproduced.

PARTICIPATION

Whether the agent enters the market and under what conditions.

BID / OFFER PRICE

Price relative to private value or cost, including persistent shading.

REVISION

How orders change after new information or market movement.

PEER DEPENDENCE

Whether visible actions by other agents change the response.

LATENCY

Timing of the action and whether delays affect participation or outcome.

MARKET OUTCOMES

Trades, volume, surplus allocation, gains from trade, and efficiency.

Known-rule agents before unfamiliar models

Programmed agents provide ground truth. Some bid at reservation value. Some use fixed shading. Some react to trend, news, or peer activity. Because the rule is known, the experimental pipeline can be tested blind.

A valid system must recover a rule when the experiments distinguish it. It must also return ambiguity when two rules are observationally equivalent. Abstention is part of the method.

Adaptive experiment selection

A fixed battery can spend calls on conditions that no longer separate the remaining explanations. An adaptive selector can choose the next feasible intervention based on which experiment is expected to distinguish the current candidates most efficiently.

The adaptive process is confined to discovery. Reserved confirmation conditions stay untouched. If the candidate model changes after the freeze, it has to earn confirmation again.

What a credible result looks like

The output is an evidence record, not a personality label.

ObservedWhat information the agent received and what action it returned.
ComparedWhich predictive explanations were tested against the same evidence.
ValidatedWhether the pattern reproduced in held-out conditions.
BoundedWhere the result stopped holding or remained non-identifiable.
