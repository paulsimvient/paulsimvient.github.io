Case study · Economics

Dynamic economic markets

Can controlled market experiments identify how an unfamiliar AI agent will act, and does that account still hold when the market changes?

This is the economics case in a Phase I white paper led by Paul Cummings, with Dale Rothman of George Mason University as research lead. It applies the same inquiry loop as Thinking Together to agents that buy and sell. It is a research design. It is not a report of completed market results.

The market

Agents see prices and the public information assigned to their condition. They react to other participants and submit orders. An order is a time, an asset, a quantity, and a price. An invalid order is recorded as invalid. The harness does not quietly turn it into a trade.

The laboratory uses two mechanisms: a continuous double auction and a sealed-bid double auction. The market keeps private buyer values and seller costs, cash and inventory, public bids and asks, completed trades, and controlled news. The true economic state stays separate from what any one agent is allowed to see. A change in news, history, or peer information can be tested without exposing private accounts.

What the experiment asks

Competing predictive models can fit the same action in one condition and then predict different actions after an intervention. The work varies information, incentives, market history, and peer conditions to see whether those differences can be told apart.

  • Identifiability Which behavioral models can actually be separated with feasible market interventions? If the available experiments cannot separate them, the result is non-identifiability, not a forced label.
  • Experimental efficiency Can an adaptive design reach the same validated conclusion with fewer model queries than a fixed factorial design or a randomized design, without weakening calibration or replication?
  • Generalization Does a model learned in one set of conditions predict behavior under held-out values and costs, another auction mechanism, and markets that contain other AI agents?

Programmed agents supply known-rule ground truth. Unfamiliar language-model agents do not. Those agents are judged by prediction, reproducibility, calibration, and generalization. The study makes no claim about an agent’s beliefs, intent, or private reasoning. Treatment effects are claims about observable response.

Discovery and confirmation

How the book’s layers show up

  5. 5

    Observations, actions, outcomes, uncertainty, and claim status remain available for inspection.

  6. 6
    Human workbench

    A researcher can inspect the experimental process and decide what the evidence supports, rejects, or cannot distinguish.

What would count as failure

The main claim is falsifiable. Adaptive experimentation should reduce query cost without weakening calibration or independent replication. If it does not, the hypothesis fails. A model that fits the training conditions and then misses reserved valuations, the second auction, or a market of several unfamiliar agents has a boundary, not a general law. Individual-agent models that cannot predict a shared market without interaction terms are reported as insufficient.

Existing market software can run continuous and sealed-bid auctions, seeded interventions, and known-rule agents. Those fixtures check the machinery. They are not empirical results about language-model traders.
