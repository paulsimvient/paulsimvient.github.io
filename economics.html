Case study · Economics

Dynamic economic markets

Experimental identification and validation of AI behavior in a controlled market laboratory — can we tell what an unfamiliar LLM agent will do when prices, information, and other agents change?

Conceptual illustration — not measured experimental results.

This case applies the inquiry loop in Thinking Together to agents that buy and sell. It is a research design for studying black-box LLM agents under controlled economic conditions. It is not a report of completed market results.

The problem

AI agents in these markets observe prices and public information, react to other agents, and submit orders that affect subsequent market state. Competing predictive models of that behavior can look plausible in a single transcript and still fail under a controlled change.

The research goal is to determine when observable economic behavior distinguishes competing predictive models — and how much experimentation is required. Models relate to response patterns we can measure. They are not claims about internal mechanism. LLMs are judged by prediction, reproducibility, calibration, and generalization. If two models remain indistinguishable, the result is non-identifiability.

What Phase I asks

  • Identifiability Which behavioral models can actually be separated with feasible market interventions? Blind recovery of known programmed decision rules, including deliberately ambiguous cases. Failure: incorrect recovery, unsupported classification, or failure to abstain when rules are observationally equivalent.
  • Experimental efficiency Can adaptive experiment selection reach the same validated conclusion with fewer model queries than fixed-factorial or randomized designs at equal budget? Failure: no improvement in cost-to-identification without loss of calibration or replication.
  • Predictive generalization Does a model learned in one set of conditions predict behavior under held-out valuations, costs, histories, another auction mechanism, and selected interface conditions? Failure: predictive performance collapses on reserved conditions.
  • Collective prediction When multiple AI agents share a market, do individual-agent models predict prices, volume, gains from trade, and efficiency — or are interaction-specific terms required?

Market laboratory

The testbed separates the true economic state from each agent’s permitted observations. Valuations, costs, accounts, auction rules, and outcomes live in the Domain layer. Experiment controls exposure to public information, news, history, and peer observations. Validation enforces a discovery / holdout boundary so confirmation is not just replaying discovery.

At least two mechanisms are used:

  • Continuous double auction (CDA)
  • Sealed-bid double auction

The market tracks private buyer values and seller costs, cash and inventory, public bids and asks, completed trades, and controlled news. The AI action is a structured order under those constraints — not free-form text treated as settlement.

Allocative efficiency compares realized gains from trade to the maximum feasible gain for that market instance. Market qualification requires mechanism and accounting verification, including a documented high-efficiency demonstration under proof-of-concept conditions.

Information as an experimental variable

The news feed is not free-form context. Truth status, economic relevance, reliability, timing, and wording can vary while private value, cash, and quantity constraints stay fixed. A signature design crosses news with price history so competing explanations of “why the agent bid that way” become separable — or are reported as non-identifiable when they cannot be separated at the available resolution and budget.

Treatment effects support claims about observable response. They do not establish an internal mechanism. Model self-description is not treated as ground truth.

Gate What acceptance requires
Market qualification Verified mechanics and accounting; documented efficiency under qualification conditions
Reference identification Known-rule recovery, calibration, and abstention when rules are observationally equivalent
Adaptive selection Equal-budget advantage versus fixed or randomized designs without weaker confirmation
LLM validation Replicated effects plus held-out predictive performance; provider failures reported separately
Generalization Prespecified performance on reserved conditions and explicit transfer limits
Collective behavior Predicted versus observed multi-agent outcomes; evidence for or against interaction terms

How this opens the black box

Unfamiliar LLMs enter as stock agents under a standardized black-box action protocol. Programmed reference agents establish that the laboratory can recover known rules before any LLM claim is entertained. Classifiers describe systematic, reproducible changes in observable economic response — predictive signatures, not psychological traits.

Every trial carries enough context to reconstruct what happened: experiment identity, discovery versus confirmation status, seed, model configuration, observations, actions, and market outcomes. Pilot and discovery runs establish feasibility; confirmation freezes outcomes, thresholds, and holdouts before independent tests.

This is the same scientific stance as the broader research program: we learn what an AI system does by designing experiments that challenge competing explanations — not by trusting what the model says about itself. See Opening the AI Black Box.
