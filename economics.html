Can we tell how an opaque agent is trading?

Use a controlled market as a laboratory. Keep what the agent knows under experimental control, compare competing behavioral explanations, and check which ones survive.

Watch the animated experiment →
1. Trust the lab 2. Control what is seen 3. Keep rival stories 4. Choose a separating test 5. Confirm or stop 6. Keep the record

1. Trust the laboratory first

Before studying an unfamiliar agent, verify the market itself: values, costs, cash, inventory, trades, and surplus. If the accounting is wrong, every later conclusion is suspect.

2. Control what the agent can see

The true economic state can stay fixed while the information shown to the agent changes — news, price history, peer activity. That turns “context” into an experimental factor.

Held constantVaried on purpose
fundamentals, accounts, quantity limitsnews, history, peer observations

3. Keep rival behavioral stories

Do not invent a hidden psychology. Compare observable explanations: reacts to news, follows recent prices, copies peers, or some interaction among them.

4. Choose the test that separates them

Spend queries where the current explanations disagree most. A persuasive story is not enough; a discriminating comparison is.

5. Confirm — or admit non-identifiability

After exploration, lock the rules. Confirm on reserved conditions. If two explanations still look the same, the honest result is that they cannot yet be told apart.

6. Keep the full evidence record

Each trial retains what was shown, what was done, what traded, and whether the run belonged to exploration or confirmation.
