Case study · Economics

Dynamic economic markets

Can controlled market experiments identify predictive models of black-box LLM trading behavior — and do those models still hold when information, incentives, and other agents change?

We do not ask an LLM what it believes. We ask whether competing accounts of its market behavior survive controlled experiments.

Conceptual illustration — not measured experimental results. This page describes a research design, not completed market findings.

This case is the economics teaching and research track behind Thinking Together: a controlled market laboratory for studying unfamiliar LLM agents as black boxes. CoAgentics supplies the experimental apparatus — domain, strategy, experiment, validation, knowledge, and a human workbench — so a researcher can design interventions, compare models, and inspect what the evidence supports.

The scientific problem

AI agents in these markets observe prices and public information, react to other agents, and submit orders that change subsequent market state. Two predictive models can fit the same action in one condition and disagree after a single controlled change.

Phase I asks when observable economic behavior distinguishes those models, and how much experimentation is required. The models relate controlled conditions to observable actions and outcomes. They make no claim about an LLM’s beliefs, intent, reasoning process, or psychology. Judgment rests on prediction, reproducibility, calibration, and generalization. If two models remain observationally equivalent, the honest result is non-identifiability.

Falsifiable questions

Hypothesis Test Failure
Identifiability Blind recovery of known programmed decision rules, including deliberately ambiguous cases Wrong recovery, unsupported classification, or no abstention when rules are equivalent
Experimental efficiency Equal-budget adaptive selection versus fixed factorial and randomized designs No cost-to-identification gain without weaker calibration or replication
Predictive generalization Held-out valuations, costs, histories, second auction mechanism, interface conditions Performance collapses on reserved conditions
Collective prediction Predicted vs. observed prices, volume, gains from trade, and efficiency with multiple AI agents Individual-agent models fail without interaction-specific terms

Market laboratory

The testbed separates the true economic state from each agent’s permitted observations. Valuations, costs, accounts, auction rules, and outcomes live in Domain. Strategy holds reference rules and competing predictive models. Experiment applies interventions. Validation keeps discovery separate from confirmation. Knowledge stores evidence and uncertainty. The Human Workbench lets a researcher inspect and approve the process.

Phase I uses at least two mechanisms:

  • Continuous double auction (CDA)
  • Sealed-bid double auction

The market tracks private buyer values and seller costs, cash and inventory, public bids and asks, completed trades, and controlled news. An AI action is a structured order under those constraints — not free-form text treated as settlement. Actions are validated before they reach the market.

Allocative efficiency compares realized gains from trade to the maximum feasible gain for that market instance. Qualification also looks at transaction prices, bid/offer distributions and shading, convergence and spread, trade volume, and surplus allocation — not efficiency alone. A reference regression market (known buyer values and seller costs with a known efficient allocation) verifies accounting before any LLM claim is entertained.

Baseline capability vs. Phase I research

Already in the apparatus What Phase I must demonstrate
Economic simulation with CDA and sealed-bid paths Qualify markets against mechanics, bidding-pattern, and high-efficiency criteria
Seeded / paired interventions and evidence logging Exercise stock agents from many distinct LLMs with retained configuration and failure evidence
Programmed-agent fixtures for known-result paths Validate response/preference classifiers on known-rule agents, then unfamiliar LLMs
Discovery / validation infrastructure and workbench Show adaptive selection reduces query cost without weakening confirmation

Information as an experimental variable

News is not free-form context. Truth status, economic relevance, reliability, timing, and wording can vary while private value, cash, and quantity constraints stay fixed. Phase I’s signature design crosses news with price history so competing explanations of “why the agent bid that way” become separable — or are reported as non-identifiable.

Cell What is held fixed What changes
Baseline Private value, cash, quantity, market fundamental Neutral news · stable history
News only Same private state Economically relevant news · stable history
History only Same private state Neutral news · rising or falling price history
News × history Same private state Relevant news and price history together

Illustrative fixture cells can show how news effects and history effects would be measured. Those numbers are software fixtures for method demonstration, not LLM findings. Treatment effects support claims about observable response. They do not establish an internal mechanism. Model self-description is never ground truth.

How an unfamiliar agent is treated

For an unfamiliar agent, behavior is an unknown stochastic policy over the observations it is allowed to see: private economic information, public market state, news, permitted history, and any allowed peer observations. Configuration records model/provider identity and, where available, version, instructions, observation format, memory policy, and inference settings. A prompt or configuration change creates a new agent condition — it is not quietly treated as the same agent.

Method order matters:

  • Qualify market mechanics with known-rule agents first
  • Recover known rules, including cases that should force abstention
  • Only then estimate predictive signatures on unfamiliar LLMs
  • Freeze models, outcomes, thresholds, and holdouts before confirmation
  • Test generalization on conditions not used to choose the model

Acceptance gates

Gate What acceptance requires
Market qualification Verified mechanics and accounting; documented efficiency under qualification conditions
Reference identification Known-rule recovery, calibration, and abstention when rules are observationally equivalent
Adaptive selection Equal-budget advantage versus fixed or randomized designs without weaker confirmation
LLM validation Replicated effects plus held-out predictive performance; provider failures reported separately
Generalization Prespecified performance on reserved conditions and explicit transfer limits
Collective behavior Predicted versus observed multi-agent outcomes; evidence for or against interaction terms

How this opens the black box

Unfamiliar LLMs enter as stock agents under a standardized black-box action protocol. Every trial carries enough context to reconstruct what happened: campaign and experiment identity, discovery versus confirmation status, seed, model configuration, observations, validated actions, and market outcomes. The independent market run or pair — not every action inside it — is the experimental unit for uncertainty and hypothesis tests.

This is the same scientific stance as Opening the AI Black Box and the Night Crossing case: decipher behavior by discovering which explanations survive controlled attempts to disprove them.
